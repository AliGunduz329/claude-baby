# Essay Architecture & Narrative Rules

## 1. Mechanics of the Opening Hook
- **Show the Scene or the Conflict Immediately:** Drop the reader into physical reality, a specific tension, or an unorthodox observation.
- **Banned Openings:**
  - Broad philosophical statements (*"Since the dawn of civilization..."*, *"Change is an inevitable reality..."*).
  - Rhetorical questions (*"Have you ever felt out of place?"*).
  - Dictionary definitions or cliché quotes.

## 2. Avoiding Fake Epiphanies
- **Earn the Conclusion:** Never wrap up an essay with a sudden moral revelation or a neat bow.
- **Show the Action, Not the Lesson:** Instead of writing *"This taught me the value of persistence,"* narrate the fourth time you had to rewrite the algorithm or rebuild the physical circuit at 2:00 AM.
- If a lesson cannot be shown through specific choices or physical details, cut it.

## 3. Sentence Craft & Cadence
- **The 3-Sentence Cadence Check:** Avoid chaining complex compound sentences with the same rhythm. Follow a detailed, multi-clause observation with a sharp declarative sentence.
- **Eliminate Moralizing:** Never preach to the reader or lecture them on what is virtuous. Present the evidence and let the reader draw the conclusion.
