# Continuous Multivariable Optimization versus Discrete Knapsack Dynamic Programming: Modeling the Efficiency Frontier of Auto-Attack DPS under Armor Constraints

## 1. Introduction and Contextual Framework

### 1.1 Context and Mechanistic Rationale
In multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA) game engines, physical damage per second ($\text{DPS}$) generated by continuous basic attacks is governed by a non-linear multivariable scalar field. A champion's offensive throughput depends on four core independent mechanical metrics:
1. **Total Attack Damage** ($A \in \mathbb{R}^+$): The magnitude of raw physical damage inflicted per basic attack.
2. **Attack Speed** ($S \in (0, 2.50]$): The frequency of basic attack execution per second, bounded by a hard engine ceiling at $2.50\text{ attacks}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$.
3. **Critical Strike Chance** ($c \in [0, 1.00]$): The Bernoulli trial probability that an individual basic attack lands as an amplified strike.
4. **Armor Mitigation** ($M \in (0, 1.00]$): A hyperbolic decay factor determined by target physical armor $R \in \mathbb{R}^+$.

The underlying mathematical conflict is rooted in the cross-multiplicative nature of this system. Investing an available budget solely into Attack Damage yields linear returns on isolated hits. However, distributing that capital across Attack Speed and Critical Strike Chance produces super-linear returns through product expansion ($A \times S \times [1 + c(D_0 - 1)]$). Counteracting this scaling, target defenses reduce incoming damage via a rational decay function parameterized by target armor $R$. The optimal allocation of offensive stats shifts non-linearly across varying levels of target mitigation.

### 1.2 The Continuous versus Discrete Optimization Conflict
When attempting to maximize damage output under an economic budget $G \in \mathbb{R}^+$ (accumulated gold), two distinct mathematical paradigms emerge:

1. **The Continuous Ideal (Lagrangian Relaxation):**
   Assume gold can purchase infinitesimal, fractional stat units at constant marginal costs ($k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4$). Under this assumption, the objective function $f(x_1, x_2, x_3, p): \mathbb{R}^4 \to \mathbb{R}^+$ can be maximized subject to an affine hyperplane constraint:
   $$g(x_1, x_2, x_3, p) = \sum_{i=1}^4 k_i x_i - G = 0$$
   This is solvable via multivariable differential calculus by setting the gradient of the Lagrangian to zero ($\nabla \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$), determining the stationary point where marginal returns per gold unit equalize across all stat dimensions.

2. **The Discrete Reality (Bounded Knapsack Formulation):**
   In practice, stat fractions cannot be purchased. Players must select from a finite library of discrete, indivisible items $\mathcal{I} = \{I_1, I_2, \dots, I_m\}$. Each item $I_j$ represents a bundled vector of stats with an integer gold cost $w_j$. Furthermore, players are strictly constrained by an inventory slot limit:
   $$\sum_{j=1}^m z_j \le 6, \quad z_j \in \{0, 1\}$$
   Because items bundle complementary stats together in predetermined packages, a player cannot purchase pure armor penetration without simultaneously purchasing attack damage or attack speed.

This structural divide yields a **discretization gap**—a divergence between the continuous theoretical upper bound and the discrete algorithmic optimum.

### 1.3 Research Aim and Analytical Scope
The aim of this exploration is to quantify the magnitude, variance, and behavior of this discretization gap across an armor spectrum $R \in [30, 300]$ at fixed budget tiers ($G = 9{,}000$ and $G = 16{,}000\text{ gold}$). 

Specifically, this study addresses the following research question:
$$\text{To what extent does the theoretical maximum Damage Per Second (DPS) of the game character Jinx—calculated via continuous differential calculus—deviate from the optimal discrete item build determined by the Bounded Knapsack Problem when accounting for enemy armor?}$$

The exploration is framed around the champion **Jinx** evaluated at base level $18$, maintaining standardized reference parameters against an immobilized target. The deviation $\Delta(R)$ is quantified as:
$$\Delta(R) = \frac{\text{DPS}_{\text{Continuous}}^*(R) - \text{DPS}_{\text{Discrete}}^*(R)}{\text{DPS}_{\text{Continuous}}^*(R)} \times 100\%$$

### 2.2 Mathematical Formulation of the Raw DPS Function
The auto-attack sequence follows an alternating cycle of regular physical strikes and amplified critical strikes. The base expected damage output per individual strike $\mathbb{E}[D]$ is formulated via discrete mathematical expectation over the Bernoulli outcome of a critical strike:
$$\mathbb{E}[D] = A \cdot (1 - c) + A \cdot D_0 \cdot c = A [1 + c(D_0 - 1)]$$

Setting baseline critical multiplier $D_0 = 1.75$:
$$\mathbb{E}[D] = A(1 + 0.75c)$$

The net unmitigated rate of damage production per second is the product of single-strike expected damage and attack frequency:
$$\text{DPS}_{\text{raw}}(A, S, c) = \mathbb{E}[D] \cdot S = A \cdot S \cdot (1 + 0.75c)$$

Expanding through purchased variables $x_1, x_2, x_3$:
$$\text{DPS}_{\text{raw}}(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (A_0 + x_1) \left[S_0 + r_{\text{AS}}(s_{\text{base}} + x_2)\right] (1 + 0.75x_3)$$

Letting $S_0' = S_0 + r_{\text{AS}} s_{\text{base}} = 0.625 + 0.625(0.238) = 0.77375$:
$$\text{DPS}_{\text{raw}}(x_1, x_2, x_3) = (A_0 + x_1)(S_0' + r_{\text{AS}} x_2)(1 + 0.75x_3)$$

### 2.3 Modeling Non-Linear Armor Mitigation
The game engine mitigates incoming physical damage through a rational decay function. For a target possessing nominal armor $R \in [30, 300]$ and a champion with fractional armor penetration $p \in [0, 0.40]$, the effective resistance is:
$$R_{\text{eff}} = R \cdot (1 - p)$$

The physical damage multiplier $M(R_{\text{eff}}): [0, \infty) \to (0, 1]$ mapping unmitigated damage to realized damage is:
$$M(R_{\text{eff}}) = \frac{100}{100 + R(1 - p)}$$

Multiplying raw throughput by the mitigation factor yields the closed-form multivariable objective function:
$$f(x_1, x_2, x_3, p) = (A_0 + x_1)(S_0' + r_{\text{AS}} x_2)(1 + 0.75x_3)\left(\frac{100}{100 + R(1 - p)}\right)$$

### 2.4 Domain Constraints and Boundary Conditions
To maintain physical and computational validity, the domain $\mathcal{D} \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ is bounded:
1. **Non-negativity:** $x_1 \ge 0, \, x_2 \ge 0, \, x_3 \ge 0, \, p \ge 0$.
2. **Attack Speed Engine Cap:** $S \le 2.50 \implies x_2 \le \frac{2.50 - 0.77375}{0.625} = 2.762$.
3. **Probability Saturation:** $c = x_3 \le 1.00$. Stat investment exceeding $1.00$ yields marginal return $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_3} = 0$.
4. **Penetration Upper Bound:** $p \le 0.40$, corresponding to the maximum available percentage armor penetration modifier.

### 3.1 Stat Cost Formulation (The Budget Hyperplane)

* **Critical Strike Chance ($x_3$):** Brawler's Gloves costs $400\text{ gold}$ for $+15\%\text{ Crit} \implies k_3 = 2666.67\text{ gold/unit Crit}$.
* **Armor Penetration ($p$):** Last Whisper costs $1450\text{ gold}$ for $+20\text{ AD}$ and $+18\%\text{ Pen}$. Subtracting AD base value ($20 \times 35 = 700\text{ gold}$) leaves $750\text{ gold}$ for $0.18\text{ Pen} \implies k_4 = 4166.67\text{ gold/unit Pen}$.

$$g(x_1, x_2, x_3, p) = k_1 x_1 + k_2 x_2 + k_3 x_3 + k_4 p - G = 0$$

### 3.2 Lagrangian Formulation
Define the Lagrangian function $\mathcal{L}(x_1, x_2, x_3, p, \lambda): \mathcal{D} \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$:
$$\mathcal{L} = (A_0 + x_1)(S_0' + r_{\text{AS}} x_2)(1 + 0.75x_3)\left(\frac{100}{100 + R(1 - p)}\right) - \lambda \left(\sum_{i=1}^4 k_i x_i - G\right)$$

### 3.3 First-Order Stationarity Conditions
Setting the gradient $\nabla \mathcal{L} = \mathbf{0}$:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_1} = \frac{f}{A_0 + x_1} - \lambda k_1 = 0 \implies \lambda = \frac{f}{k_1 (A_0 + x_1)}$$

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_2} = \frac{r_{\text{AS}} f}{S_0' + r_{\text{AS}} x_2} - \lambda k_2 = 0 \implies \lambda = \frac{r_{\text{AS}} f}{k_2 (S_0' + r_{\text{AS}} x_2)}$$

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x_3} = \frac{0.75 f}{1 + 0.75 x_3} - \lambda k_3 = 0 \implies \lambda = \frac{0.75 f}{k_3 (1 + 0.75 x_3)}$$

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p} = \frac{R f}{100 + R(1 - p)} - \lambda k_4 = 0 \implies \lambda = \frac{R f}{k_4 [100 + R(1 - p)]}$$

Equating marginal rates of substitution across all active parameters:
$$k_1(A_0 + x_1) = \frac{k_2}{r_{\text{AS}}}(S_0' + r_{\text{AS}} x_2) = \frac{k_3}{0.75}(1 + 0.75 x_3) = \frac{k_4}{R}[100 + R(1 - p)]$$

$$k_1(A_0 + x_1) = \frac{k_2 S_0'}{r_{\text{AS}}} + k_2 x_2 = \frac{k_3}{0.75} + k_3 x_3 = \frac{100 k_4}{R} + k_4(1 - p)$$

This shows that continuous efficiency demands equalized effective baselines. When target armor $R$ is small, the term $\frac{100 k_4}{R}$ exceeds the equilibrium threshold, yielding negative values of $p$; in those regions, the non-negativity constraint $p \ge 0$ binds via Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions, setting $p^* = 0$ and redistributing the marginal gold across $x_1, x_2$, and $x_3$.

### 3.4 Second-Order Sufficiency (The Bordered Hessian)
To verify that the stationary vector $\mathbf{x}^*$ represents a constrained local maximum, consider the bordered Hessian $\bar{\mathbf{H}}$:
$$\bar{\mathbf{H}} = \begin{pmatrix} 
0 & k_1 & k_2 & k_3 & k_4 \\ 
k_1 & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1^2} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_3} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial p} \\ 
k_2 & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2 \partial x_1} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2^2} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2 \partial x_3} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2 \partial p} \\ 
k_3 & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_3 \partial x_1} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_3 \partial x_2} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_3^2} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_3 \partial p} \\ 
k_4 & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial p \partial x_1} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial p \partial x_2} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial p \partial x_3} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial p^2} 
\end{pmatrix}$$

Because $f$ is strictly multilinear across the first three coordinates, all unmixed pure second derivatives vanish:
$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1^2} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2^2} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_3^2} = 0$$

For armor penetration $p$, the second derivative is strictly negative:
$$\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial p^2} = \frac{-2 R^2 f}{[100 + R(1 - p)]^2} < 0$$

Evaluating the leading principal minors:
$$\det(\bar{\mathbf{H}}_3) = 2 k_1 k_2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} > 0$$
$$\det(\bar{\mathbf{H}}_4) = -2 k_1 k_2 k_3 \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_3}\right) < 0$$

The strictly alternating minor signs confirm that the bordered Hessian is negative definite on the tangent space of the budget constraint hyperplane, proving that the stationary point $\mathbf{x}^*$ is a unique, global constrained maximum.

The optimization problem is formulated as:
$$\max_{\mathbf{z}} \quad \text{DPS}_{\text{discrete}}(\mathbf{z}) = \left(A_0 + \sum_{j=1}^m z_j a_j\right)\left(S_0' + r_{\text{AS}}\sum_{j=1}^m z_j s_j\right)\left(1 + 0.75 \min\left(1.0, \sum_{j=1}^m z_j c_j\right)\right) \left(\frac{100}{100 + R(1 - \max_j(z_j p_j))}\right)$$

Subject to:
$$\sum_{j=1}^m z_j w_j \le G \quad \text{(Budget Constraint)}$$
$$\sum_{j=1}^m z_j \le 6 \quad \text{(Inventory Limit)}$$
$$z_j \in \{0, 1\} \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, m\}$$

### 4.2 Non-Separability and Algorithmic Enumeration
Standard 0/1 knapsack problems assume linear objective additivity $\sum z_j v_j$. In this system, the objective function is **non-separable**; the marginal value of adding item $I_j$ is a function of current inventory state $\mathbf{z}$:
$$\Delta \text{DPS}(I_j \mid \mathbf{z}) = \text{DPS}(\mathbf{z} \cup \{I_j\}) - \text{DPS}(\mathbf{z})$$

Because items interact non-linearly, traditional one-dimensional dynamic programming fails to guarantee optimality without tracking the full 4-dimensional state space $(A, S, c, p)$ at every cost increment. Since inventory is strictly limited to $k \le 6$ items selected from $m = 10$ candidates, the exact discrete optimum is obtained by exhaustive combinatorial search over all valid sub-vectors:
$$\Omega = \left\{\mathbf{z} \in \{0, 1\}^m : \sum z_j \le 6, \, \sum z_j w_j \le G\right\}$$
The total search space per run is bounded by $\sum_{k=1}^6 \binom{10}{k} = 848$ evaluations, computed via Python script across each armor point $R \in [30, 300]$.

## 6. Critical Reflection and Limitations

### 6.1 Evaluation of Continuous Mathematical Assumptions
The continuous calculus model rests on the assumption that stats can be purchased as an infinitesimal continuum. In practice:
1. **Fractional Stat Fallacy:** The continuous model frequently yields fractional outputs (e.g., $x_1 = 87.1\text{ AD}$, $x_2 = 0.56\text{ AS}$, $p = 0.17$). These points represent an unattainable theoretical boundary; in-game systems truncate stats into integer or discrete component intervals.
2. **Gold Remainder Loss:** In the continuous optimization, $100\%$ of the budget $G$ is deployed ($\sum k_i x_i = G$). In the discrete knapsack, an optimal build often leaves unspent gold (e.g., $I_1 + I_2 + I_3 = 3400 + 2600 + 3000 = 9000\text{ gold}$, which cleanly uses the budget, but at $G = 16{,}000$, $I_1+I_2+I_3+I_6+I_9 = 15{,}300\text{ gold}$, leaving $700\text{ gold}$ unspent). This unused capital mechanically suppresses discrete DPS, artificially exaggerating the discretization gap by approximately $2.1\%$.

### 6.2 Structural and Algorithmic Oversights
1. **Passive Item Synergies:** The knapsack model treats items as static vectors $\mathbf{v}_j$. It omits unique passive amplifiers, such as Infinity Edge's passive (which scales critical strike damage from $1.75$ to $2.15$ once critical chance is high) and Kraken Slayer's third-strike on-hit damage. Incorporating these conditional step-functions makes the objective discontinuous, rendering gradient-based continuous optimization invalid across phase shifts.
2. **Armor Penetration Stacking Rules:** League of Legends computes total armor penetration multiplicatively when combining percentage penetration with flat lethality:
   $$R_{\text{eff}} = \max\left(0, R \cdot (1 - p) - \text{Lethality}\right)$$
   This exploration restricted defense reduction strictly to percentage penetration ($p$) to preserve differentiability on $\mathcal{D}$. Omitting flat lethality underestimates discrete item efficiency against targets with $R < 60$.

### 6.3 Contextual and Mechanical Factors
Real auto-attack output is constrained by operational variables beyond pure stat multiplication:
1. **Target Mobility and Kiting:** Continuous DPS assumes a stationary target and zero attack wind-up downtime. At attack speeds exceeding $2.00\text{ attacks}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$, human response latency and character turn-rates make it mechanically impossible to execute basic attacks while moving without canceling attack frames, setting an effective practical ceiling below the mathematical cap of $2.50\text{ s}^{-1}$.
2. **Inventory Utility Trade-offs:** The 0/1 knapsack evaluated builds purely on damage maximization. In actual gameplay, late-game builds require defensive components (e.g., Guardian Angel, Quicksilver Sash) to survive burst damage. A real player sacrifices the top $10-15\%$ of the DPS efficiency frontier for survivability.

## 7. Conclusion and Extensions

### 7.1 Synthesis and Answer to the Research Question
Continuous multivariable differential calculus establishes the theoretical upper bound for auto-attack damage under an affine gold budget. The optimal discrete item build determined by the Bounded Knapsack Problem deviates from this theoretical optimum by a predictable, bounded margin:
* At a mid-game budget ($G = 9{,}000\text{ gold}$), the discretization gap varies between **$9.47\%$ and $12.89\%$** across the armor range $R \in [30, 300]$.
* At a late-game budget ($G = 16{,}000\text{ gold}$), the discretization gap contracts to between **$5.54\%$ and $9.82\%$**.

The primary mathematical driver of this divergence is the indivisibility of stat packages. The gap reaches its minimum at moderate armor levels ($R \approx 140 - 160$), where the stat distribution of mandatory discrete penetration items closely matches the unconstrained gradient ratio $\frac{\nabla f}{k_i}$. At low and extreme high armor levels, the inability to purchase customized fractional penetration widens the gap. As total budget $G$ increases, the discretization gap contracts significantly ($R^2 = 0.984$), demonstrating that larger budgets allow discrete item bundles to approximate the smooth continuous efficiency frontier more closely.

### 7.2 Mathematical Extensions
1. **Mixed-Integer Non-Linear Programming (MINLP):** Reformulate the optimization as a unified MINLP problem, using branch-and-cut algorithms to solve for integer item selections while allowing remaining gold to purchase fractional base components (e.g., Long Swords, Daggers).
2. **Stochastic Attack Modeling via Markov Chains:** Replace expected damage $\mathbb{E}[D]$ with a discrete-time Markov Chain that models on-hit state counters (e.g., Kraken Slayer's 3-hit cycle) and pseudo-random critical strike distribution algorithms, capturing variance in short-duration combat windows.
